How to Play Sudoku

  1. Choose a difficulty to begin: Easy, Medium or Hard. The harder the level, the fewer starting numbers (givens) you receive. A fresh puzzle with a unique solution is generated instantly.
  2. Tap any empty cell to select it — the board highlights its row, column and 3×3 box, plus every matching number — then tap a digit on the number pad to place it.
  3. The goal is to fill the grid so that every row, every column and every 3×3 box contains the digits 1 to 9 exactly once.
  4. If you place a wrong digit it flashes red and counts as a mistake, so you can correct it straight away.
  5. Toggle Notes mode to pencil small candidate digits into a cell while you are still deciding, and use Erase to clear a cell completely.
  6. Tap Check Board at any time to verify your progress. Fill the final cell correctly to finish — your time is compared with your saved best for that difficulty.

Pro Winning Tips

  • Start with the nearly-full units. Scan every row, column and box and fill in the "naked singles" — cells where only one digit can possibly go. Easy puzzles often half-solve themselves this way.
  • Use notes generously. Before making decisions, pencil the possible candidates into the empty cells of a box. Patterns that are invisible in your head become obvious on the grid.
  • Hunt for hidden singles. Sometimes a digit can only fit in one cell of a row, column or box even though that cell has several candidates. This is the single most useful technique beyond the basics.
  • Tap a placed number to highlight all its twins. It is the fastest way to see where that digit still can and cannot go across the whole board.
  • Never guess. Every puzzle here has exactly one solution and it can always be reached by logic. If you feel forced to guess, you have missed a deduction — scan again instead.
  • Work box by box when stuck. If a full-board scan stalls, focus on one 3×3 box and cross-reference its missing digits against its rows and columns.

Sudoku Strategy

Solid Sudoku strategy is systematic elimination. Begin with cross-hatching: for each digit from 1 to 9, sweep the rows and columns that already contain it and mark where it can still live inside each 3×3 box — this produces most of your early placements and notes. Once the obvious singles dry up, shift to candidate logic. A naked pair — two cells in the same row, column or box holding the exact same two candidates — means those two digits are locked into those cells, so you can erase them from every other cell in that unit, often triggering a chain of new singles. Pointing pairs work in the other direction: if a digit inside a box can only sit in one row, it can be eliminated from the rest of that row outside the box. Manage your mistakes deliberately: because this game flags wrong entries immediately, use that as a safety net for checking a deduction, not as a licence to guess — clean solves are what improve your best times. Difficulty is mostly about clue count and how deep the logic chains run: Easy rewards scanning, Medium demands full notes, and Hard regularly requires pairs and pointing techniques. Finish with a full verification pass using Check Board before filling the last few cells.
