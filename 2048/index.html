How to Play 2048

  1. Tap New Game to start. Two tiles, each worth 2 or 4, appear on the empty 4×4 grid.
  2. Slide every tile at once using the arrow keys on a keyboard, a swipe on touch screens, or the on-screen arrow pad under the board.
  3. When two tiles with the same number crash into each other, they merge into a single tile worth double: 2 + 2 becomes 4, 4 + 4 becomes 8, 8 + 8 becomes 16, and so on.
  4. After every successful slide, one new tile (usually a 2, sometimes a 4) appears in a random empty square — so the board keeps filling up.
  5. Keep merging upward through 32, 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1024 until two 1024 tiles combine into the golden 2048 tile. That is the win.
  6. If the grid fills completely and no two neighbouring tiles match, no move is possible and the game ends. Your final score is the total of every merge you made, and your best score is saved for next time.

Pro Winning Tips

  • Pick a corner and protect it. Keep your biggest tile anchored in one corner — most players choose bottom-left or bottom-right — and never make a move that drags it out of that corner.
  • Play in three directions only. If your corner is at the bottom, slide left, right and down, and avoid pressing up unless there is genuinely no other move. The forbidden direction is what destroys most good boards.
  • Build a staircase. Arrange your tiles in descending order snaking away from the corner — 1024 in the corner, then 512, 256, 128 beside it — so small merges cascade into big ones in a single slide.
  • Keep your home row full. A completely filled bottom row cannot shift sideways, which keeps your carefully ordered chain in place while the upper rows stay fluid.
  • Merge small tiles first. Do not chase one spectacular merge in the middle of the board; tidy up the 2s, 4s and 8s so they feed the chain instead of clogging the grid.
  • Slow down past half-full. Once more than eight squares are occupied, think two moves ahead before sliding. One careless move can bury your biggest tile under junk with no way back.
  • If you must break the corner, rebuild it immediately. Treat restoring the corner as your very next job, before any other merge.

2048 Strategy

The corner strategy works because 2048 is really a game about order, not arithmetic. Every slide moves every tile, so the only way to stay in control is to make one edge of the board behave predictably. With your largest tile pinned in a corner and values descending along the edge like a staircase, most slides either feed the chain or shuffle the loose upper tiles harmlessly. Think of the board in two zones: the "vault" (your corner and its staircase, which you never disturb) and the "workshop" (the remaining squares, where new tiles are combined before being fed into the vault). Experienced players also watch the tile count, not just the score: keeping at least three or four empty squares gives you room to recover from an unlucky spawn. In the endgame, when 1024 sits in the corner, protect the 512 next to it at all costs — losing the second-biggest tile usually means the chain collapses before the winning merge. Reaching 2048 typically takes 8 to 15 focused minutes, and players who master the corner method win far more often than players who slide randomly. Push past the win and the same method scales toward 4096: the staircase simply grows one step longer.
